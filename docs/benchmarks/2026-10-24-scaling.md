## Results

| N | startup `full_s` | peak RSS | lookup p50 | lookup p99 | multicall p50 (12 cols) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 0.122 | 21.0 MB | 0.070 ms | 0.172 ms | 13.7 ms |
| 5,000 | 0.556 | 57.6 MB | 0.069 ms | 0.165 ms | 75.5 ms |
| 14,000 | 1.375 | 137.5 MB | 0.070 ms | 0.165 ms | 216.9 ms |

Peak thread count was 11 and peak FD count 12 at every size (no rechecks were
queued). `cpu_s` at 14k was 1.55 s.

## Reading

- **Startup is linear.** 14k/1k = 11.2x for 14x the entries, with RSS at
  ~9.8 KB/torrent. The previous quadratic term (per-view `iter().any` /
  `position` scans) is gone; the remaining cost is insertion plus resume and
  directory handling.
- **Lookup is flat.** p99 is identical at 1k and 14k, including 10% random
  misses. The hash index is doing its job; no scan remains in `d.hash`.
- **Multicall is linear in rows, not squared.** 14k rows x 12 columns costs
  ~217 ms, i.e. ~1.3 us per (row x column). It does not grow super-linearly,
  but it is the largest per-poll cost and the Phase 7 target (per-command
  parse hoisting).
- **Threads and FDs are flat at rest.** 11 threads / 12 FDs at 14k. This is the
  baseline the recheck benchmark's `peak_threads` must be compared against.

## Reproduce

```bash
scripts/bench/run_all.sh --count 14000 --lookups 5000 --multicall 10
```

Caveats: sparse/absent payloads (no hashing), ext4 on a local SSD, a single
idle host, and a release build. Absolute numbers will differ on FUSE/`shfs`;
see `README.md`.
